BREAKING
PUP Sto. Tomas COMELEC adopts presidential conclave to select next CSC president
The Cloud Sentinel
Published Nov 20, 2026 11:00:00 AM PHT

STO TOMAS, BATANGAS | The Commission on Elections of the Polytechnic University of the Philippines Sto. Tomas Campus has formally adopted a presidential conclave as the official mechanism for selecting the next Central Student Council president for Academic Year 2026-2027, following a 13-2 vote among commissioners.

In Resolution No. 004, Series of 2026, adopted on November 20, the Commission rejected plurality voting in favor of a presidential conclave to resolve the contested CSC presidency following the November 6 student elections.

The decision came after neither of the two presidential candidates secured the prescribed 50-percent vote requirement. Based on the initial and unofficial tally released on November 7, Christopher R. Gutierrez received 715 votes, while Kent Simon C. Escobar garnered 606 votes. Meanwhile, 699 voters chose to abstain, out of 2,020 valid ballots.

Under Section 53.1 of the Electoral Code, a candidate running unopposed is required to obtain the prescribed 50-percent vote threshold. However, the Commission identified a gap in the existing provisions, noting that the Code does not expressly establish a corresponding procedure for determining the winner of a contested presidential position under the circumstances presented.

The substantial number of abstentions also became part of the Commission's deliberations. However, the resolution clarified that abstention, as defined under Section 3.1 of the Electoral Code, represents a voter's decision not to select any candidate and does not constitute an affirmative vote against either presidential candidate.

DIVIDED POSITIONS ON THE PRESIDENTIAL SELECTION

Before reaching its decision, the Commission convened an En Banc session to deliberate on two proposed courses of action: plurality voting and a presidential conclave.

Commissioners supporting plurality voting argued that the votes cast during the election must be recognized and that the absence of an absolute majority should not automatically constitute a failure of election. They also raised concerns about introducing a different selection mechanism and a new pool of prospective candidates after students had already exercised their right to vote.

On the other hand, commissioners supporting the presidential conclave maintained that proceeding with the candidate who received the highest number of votes would not necessarily establish broader confidence among the student body, particularly given the number of abstentions.

They proposed involving the Student Leaders of College Departments (SLCDs), citing their existing leadership experience, organizational responsibilities, and representation of their respective academic departments.

The resolution also documented the consultative votes of the SLCD Presidents and incumbent CSC President Edwil Grae Zapata. Their votes favored plurality voting, with 11 votes, against eight votes for the presidential conclave.

Despite the consultative results, the official COMELEC En Banc vote reached a different outcome, with 13 commissioners voting in favor of the presidential conclave and two supporting plurality voting. The Commission emphasized that the consultative votes were considered additional institutional perspectives and were not combined with or treated as substitutes for the official En Banc results.

SLCD PRESIDENTS TO FORM PROPOSED POOL OF PRESIDENTIAL CANDIDATES

Under the adopted resolution, the presidential conclave will involve SLCD Presidents as the proposed pool of prospective candidates for the CSC presidency.

Their participation, however, will be subject to screening, evaluation, and verification of their eligibility, qualifications, willingness to serve, and capacity to fulfill the responsibilities of the position.

The Commission will also establish implementing guidelines for the conclave, covering nomination and screening procedures, the composition of the voting body, quorum requirements, voting thresholds, and balloting procedures.

These guidelines are intended to ensure an orderly, transparent, and accountable selection process. The resolution does not yet establish the specific date or complete mechanics of the conclave.

The Commission further clarified that adopting the presidential conclave does not mean that abstentions are votes against either candidate. Neither does the decision constitute an automatic finding of misconduct, incapacity, or disqualification on the part of Gutierrez or Escobar.

IMPLICATIONS FOR STUDENT GOVERNANCE

The November 6 elections recorded 2,020 valid ballots. While the presidential position had two candidates, the remaining positions, from the vice presidency and councilorships to college representatives, had only one candidate each.

Under the applicable vote requirements, only the vice president, two councilors, and some college representatives were elected, leaving other positions unresolved.

The Central Student Council serves as the highest form of student governance at PUP Sto. Tomas Campus. Its president also represents the campus in ANAK PUP, the alliance of student councils across the PUP System.

With the adoption of Resolution No. 004, the selection of the next CSC president will proceed through a mechanism different from the original student election, subject to the Electoral Code, applicable University regulations, and any lawful review or challenge.

The Commission's resolution now establishes the presidential conclave as its official course of action, with the implementing guidelines and subsequent selection process yet to follow.
